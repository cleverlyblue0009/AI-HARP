## 1. Ensemble size, member training — and whether the ensemble is used at all

* `configs/agent.yaml:131-136`: `confidence_gate: {enabled: true, method: entropy,
  tau: 0.5, fallback_policy: weighted_p, ensemble_size: 5}`.
* **The ensemble is never built or used.** `ensemble_size` is read by no code
  (`grep -rn ensemble_size --include=*.py` finds only the YAML). The gate's
  `ensemble` path (`agents/confidence.py:ensemble_confidence`) is reached only
  when `method == "ensemble"` *and* member probabilities are passed;
  `agents/ai_harp.py:477` calls `self.gate.evaluate(probs)` with no members.
  No code trains ensemble members (no bootstrap, no seed ensemble, no MC
  dropout at inference).
* **What every reported result actually used:** the *entropy* gate,
  `confidence = 1 − H(p)/ln(n_actions)` (`agents/confidence.py:
  normalised_entropy_confidence`), on the single PPO policy's action
  distribution; fall back to `weighted_p` when `confidence < tau`.
  `tau = 0` disables the gate (`ConfidenceGate.evaluate`).
* Consequence for the paper: any text describing ensemble members, their
  training, or ensemble "spread" describes code that never ran.

## 2. Exact definition of "spread" in the confidence gate

Only defined for the (unused) ensemble method, `agents/confidence.py:62-83`:

    spread     = max over actions a of  std_over_members( p_m(a) )   (population std, ddof=0)
    confidence = clip(1 − spread / 0.5, 0, 1)

0.5 is the largest attainable std of a probability (half the members certain
of one action, half of another). The **maximum** over actions is used, not the
mean (the mean of 9 per-action stds reports 0.78 for maximal disagreement).
With fewer than 2 members it falls back to the entropy confidence.
The gate actually used (entropy) has no "spread".

## 3. How the 4 attention heads become the relay ranking (Fig. 4)

* Encoder: 3 × `GATv2Conv(h, h // heads, heads=4, concat=True, edge_dim=…)`
  (`agents/gat_drl.py:96-110`); hidden_dim must be divisible by heads.
* Only the **final** layer's attention is used. It is requested with
  `return_attention_weights=True` and **averaged over heads**:
  `alpha = alpha.mean(dim=1)` (`agents/gat_drl.py:134-136`).
* `relay_ranking` (`agents/gat_drl.py:151-174`): each node's score is that
  head-averaged attention weight on its edge **into the holder**; nodes with no
  such edge get −inf. `relay_top_1/2/3` designate the 1st/2nd/3rd most-attended
  neighbour (`RELAY_ACTIONS`, `agents/gat_drl.py:55`).
* GCN/MLP/star ablations have no attention → ranking is all zeros → `relay_top_k`
  picks by node order (documented handicap, `relay_ranking` docstring).

## 4. Why rural nominal range is 610 m and urban LOS is 480 m

Same radio in both: EIRP 23 dBm, sensitivity −85 dBm, noise −95 dBm, two-ray
breakpoint `d_bp = 4·h_t·h_r/λ = 177.1 m` (1.5 m antennas, 5.9 GHz). Only the
dual-slope exponents differ (`configs/phy.yaml` `path_loss.exponent` /
`exponent_far`):

| scenario | n1 (≤ d_bp) | n2 (> d_bp) | nominal range (sensitivity) | SINR-limited range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rural_highway | 1.9 | 3.8 | **610.4 m** | 689.0 m |
| urban_grid / urban_nlos (LOS) | 2.0 | 4.2 | **479.6 m** | 535.2 m |

Measured with `sim.phy.build_phy(...).summary()` (clear weather) and identical
to the `comm_range_m` column of every row of `results/runs.csv`
(rural 608.8–610.4 m, urban 478.7–479.6 m across weathers — the 0.24%
weather effect). urban_nlos additionally applies building blocking (NLOS
corner loss), which gives the README's 101 m blocked-link range.

**Stale number:** `configs/phy.yaml:60` and several README passages say the
nominal range is **562 m**; the code computes 610.4 m. 562 m is not produced
by the current configuration.

## 6. What a "cell" is

The code uses "cell" for **(scenario, density, weather, hazard_type)**
(`analysis/comparator.py: CellKey`; `analysis/grid_stats.py: CELL`).

* `results/runs.csv`: 3 scenarios × 8 densities × 4 weathers × 5 hazards =
  **480 cells**, × 10 seeds × (9 baselines + agent at τ=0 and τ=0.5) = 52,800 rows.
* Matched-coverage (Algorithm 1) cells: only the **4** headline cells in
  `configs/experiment.yaml: comparator.cells` — all clear weather, fog_bank.
* "24 cells" (e.g. Fig. 9's "dearest in 15 of 24 cells") = 3 scenarios ×
  8 densities, i.e. scenario × density **with weather and hazard pooled**.
* Training multipliers are per **group** = (scenario, density), 16 groups,
  weather and hazard pooled (`agent.yaml: objective.lambda_group_by`).

## 7. How many weather conditions exist

**Four** in the evaluation factorial: clear, moderate_rain, heavy_rain,
dense_fog (`configs/experiment.yaml: sweep.weather`). **Two** are used in
training (`agent.yaml: train_weather: [clear, heavy_rain]`) and two are held
out (`held_out_weather: [moderate_rain, dense_fog]`). So "2" (§VI) is the
training set; Fig. 15's four is the full evaluation set. Weather acts only
through traffic (speed/headway factors); worst-case channel effect is 0.24%
of range.

## 8. What the "26-test family" refers to

It is not a family that was tested; it is the **threshold** below which a
Holm-corrected family can still reject at 10 paired seeds
(`analysis/grid_stats.py:21-46, 124-125`): the smallest two-sided exact
Wilcoxon p with n = 10 is 2·2⁻¹⁰ = 2⁻⁹ = 0.00195, and Holm's first step needs
`m · p_min < 0.05`. 0.00195 × 25 = 0.0488 < 0.05 but 0.00195 × 26 = 0.0508 >
0.05, so **any family of 26 or more tests is unrejectable at any effect
size**. That is why families are per cell (4 metrics → 0.0078) and no
correction across cells is applied.
